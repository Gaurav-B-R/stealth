What Rilono AI does for study-permit applicants

Rilono AI isn't a generic chatbot. It's grounded in your data — your profile, your uploaded documents, and exactly where you are in the study-permit process — so its guidance is specific to you. Its core job is mismatch-first review: before it suggests your next step, it cross-checks your profile and documents for contradictions (a name spelled differently on your passport and Letter of Acceptance, a funding gap, a missing page) and flags those first. That's the difference between "here's some general advice" and "your proof of funds is short of the current living-cost requirement — here's exactly how much more to show."

New to Rilono? Start with the Canada study-permit page, then create a free account. This guide assumes you're applying for a Canadian study permit.

One important update: the Student Direct Stream (SDS) was closed by IRCC on 8 November 2024. All study-permit applications now go through the single regular stream — so this guide (and Rilono) focus on getting that one application complete and consistent. Always confirm current rules on the official IRCC source.

Step 4 — Chat with Rilono AI the right way

Rilono AI already has your profile, documents and current stage in context — you don't need to re-explain your situation. Because it reviews for mismatches first, the most valuable way to use it is to ask it to check, not just to tell. Prompts that work well:

"Cross-check my passport, LOA and PAL for any name, date or DLI-number mismatches before I submit."

"Based on my documents, what is my single next step and what am I still missing?"

"Does my proof of funds meet the current living-cost requirement on top of my first-year tuition? What would a visa officer question?"

"Do I need a Provincial Attestation Letter for my program and province?"

Ask one focused question at a time, and act on the flagged mismatches before asking "what's next" — fixing inconsistencies early is exactly what prevents a refusal over an avoidable gap.

Step 5 — Sharpen your statement of purpose and study plan

Canadian study permits are largely decided on the strength and consistency of your file — and your statement of purpose / study plan carries real weight. Use Rilono AI to draft, critique and strengthen it so your course choice, finances and post-study intentions line up. If your application does trigger an interview, Rilono's interview-prep sessions and AI mock interviews (including full voice mock interviews on the Visa Success Pass) let you rehearse and get scored, specific feedback.

Efficient loop: draft your study plan, ask Rilono to critique it against your documents, fix the weakest points, and re-check. A consistent story across your SOP, finances and LOA is what officers are looking for.

Seven habits that make Rilono efficient

  1. Complete your profile first. Personalization is only as good as the data behind it.
  2. Upload early, not the night before. The AI can only catch a mismatch it can see — give it your documents while there's still time to fix things.
  3. Fix flags before asking "what's next." Resolve contradictions the moment Rilono surfaces them.
  4. Keep names, dates and numbers identical across your passport, LOA, PAL and IMM forms — the single most common cause of trouble.
  5. Run a Red-Flag scan before you submit your application, and again if you update any document.
  6. Make your study plan consistent. Your SOP, finances and course choice should tell one coherent story.
  7. Let the journey tracker drive. Trust the "next best action" instead of guessing the order of steps.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Student Direct Stream (SDS) still available?+

No. IRCC closed the SDS on 8 November 2024, and all study-permit applications now go through the single regular stream. Rilono focuses on making that one application complete, consistent and well-evidenced. Always confirm the current process on the official IRCC source.

Do I need a Provincial Attestation Letter (PAL)?+

Most study-permit applicants now need a PAL/TAL, though some categories — such as certain master's and doctoral students at public institutions — may be exempt, and rules change. Ask Rilono AI to check your program and province, and confirm with your institution and the official source.

How much money do I have to show?+

IRCC sets a minimum living-cost figure (updated regularly — recently around CA$22,895 for a single applicant) that you must show on top of your first-year tuition. Rilono keeps your checklist pointed at the current requirement and AI-checks your bank, GIC and sponsor documents against it. Always confirm the latest number on the official IRCC source before you rely on it.

Will Rilono fill or submit my IMM forms for me?+

No. Rilono Copilot is read-only — it guides you field by field but never types or submits. You stay in control of every field on your IRCC application.

Does Rilono give legal or immigration advice?+

No. Rilono provides document-organization and AI guidance tools, not legal or immigration advice. Canadian immigration rules change often — always confirm requirements with the official Government of Canada source.

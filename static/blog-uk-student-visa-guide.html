Two rule changes to plan around

Two changes to the Student route affect people applying now, and neither shows up on your document checklist. Decide where you stand on both before you commit to an intake.

Dependants: most students can no longer bring family

Since 1 January 2024, students on taught master's courses cannot bring dependants — a partner or children — to the UK. The route stays open only to students on PhD or research programmes, and to government-sponsored students on longer courses. If you are applying for a one-year taught master's, plan on coming alone, and don't build a family timeline around a dependant application you can't make. Check who counts as a dependant and the conditions that apply to you on GOV.UK: Student visa — your partner and children.

The Graduate route is shortening

The Graduate route is the post-study work permission you apply for after your course, and it is being cut from 2 years to 18 months for applications made from 1 January 2027. If you are choosing an intake now, work out which side of that date you would be applying — a later start can mean six fewer months of post-study work. Confirm the current length and eligibility on GOV.UK: Graduate visa before you plan around it.

Neither of these is a document you can fix later. Both shape which course and intake you choose, so raise them with Rilono AI while you're still comparing offers — not after your CAS is issued.

Seven habits that make Rilono efficient

  1. Complete your profile first. Personalization is only as good as the data behind it.
  2. Upload early, not the night before. The AI can only catch a mismatch it can see — give it your documents while there's still time to fix things.

  5. Respect the 28-day rule precisely. Keep the required balance for 28 unbroken days, never dipping below it, and make sure that 28-day period ends no more than 31 days before the date you apply — the rule is set out on GOV.UK. Then run a Red-Flag scan before you submit.
  6. Rehearse your credibility answers so your course, finances and intentions tell one coherent story.
  7. Let the journey tracker drive. Trust the "next best action" instead of guessing the order of steps.

Frequently asked questions

Is it still called a Tier 4 visa?+

No. The "Tier 4 (General)" student visa was renamed the "Student visa" in October 2020. You may still see "Tier 4" in older guides, but the current route is simply the Student visa. Always confirm on GOV.UK.

Do I still get a BRP (Biometric Residence Permit)?+

No. Physical BRPs have been replaced by eVisas — a digital record of your immigration status. After approval you travel on a short-validity entry vignette in your passport, then register for a UKVI account to view and share your eVisa. Confirm the current process on GOV.UK.

How much money do I need to show, and what is the 28-day rule?+

You must show maintenance funds — currently £1,529 a month if you study in London or £1,171 a month elsewhere in the UK, for up to 9 months, plus any unpaid course fees — held in your account for 28 consecutive days, with that 28-day period ending no more than 31 days before the date you apply, and the balance never dropping below the required amount. Rilono checks your statement against the rule. The amounts are revised periodically, so take them from GOV.UK: Student visa — money rather than from any guide.

Will Rilono fill or submit my visa application for me?+

No. Rilono Copilot is read-only — it guides you field by field but never types or submits. You stay in control of every field on your application.

Does Rilono give legal or immigration advice?+

No. Rilono provides document-organization and AI guidance tools, not legal or immigration advice. UK immigration rules change often — always confirm requirements on GOV.UK.
